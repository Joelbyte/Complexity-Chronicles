The hyperclique conjecture falls

For every fixed \(k\) there is an \(\varepsilon_k > 0\) such that \(k\)-hypercliques in an \(n\)-vertex \((k-1)\)-uniform hypergraph can be counted in \(O(n^{k-\varepsilon_k})\) time, unconditionally; as corollaries, Max-\(k\)SAT runs in \(2^{(1-\varepsilon)n}\cdot\mathrm{poly}(m,n)\) time and length-\(k\) arithmetic progressions are detected in \(n^{2-\varepsilon}\) time, with \(\varepsilon\) depending on \(k\).

ETH falls. Brute force keeps its job

Two analyses of the claimed October 2026 refutations of ETH find that the subexponential #3-SAT bounds certify no speed-up over brute force for any bounded-density input that could ever be written down, cut one algorithm's crossover point to about \(2^{420}\) clauses at clause density one, and observe that the Sparse Max-3-SAT Hypothesis falls too; a third paper, an AI-assisted exposition, explains how the counting procedure works.

4n! Gate Elimination Smashes Through

Mishra and Santhanam prove that every Boolean circuit over the full binary basis \(B_2\) computing a certain explicit function in \(\mathrm{P}\) needs at least \(4n-O(\log n)\) gates, up from the \(3.5n-o(n)\) in the paper's own earlier version.
